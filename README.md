## 🌟 Today's Stoic Reflection (2026-09-12)

As the Roman Stoic philosopher Seneca wisely observed, "We suffer more often in imagination than in reality."

Most of the anxiety we experience isn't caused by our present circumstances, but by the catastrophic scenarios our minds construct about the future. By agonizing over these hypothetical problems, we subject ourselves to real emotional pain for events that may never actually happen. Stoicism teaches us to anchor our attention in the present moment, carefully distinguishing between concrete facts and fictional fears.

**Today’s Actionable Exercise: The Reality Audit**

Whenever you feel a spike of anxiety today, pause and draw a line down a piece of paper. On the left side, write down your fear ("What if I fail this presentation?"). On the right side, write down the objective facts of your immediate present ("I am sitting at my desk, I am prepared, and nothing bad is happening right now"). Force your mind to deal only with the reality on the right, and leave the imagination on the left behind.
