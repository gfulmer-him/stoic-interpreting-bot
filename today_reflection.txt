"We suffer more often in imagination than in reality." — Seneca

Seneca reminds us that much of the anxiety we experience doesn't stem from what is currently happening, but from the catastrophic scenarios we construct in our minds. We routinely double our pain by worrying about future troubles that may never materialize, turning a simple unknown into an exhausting mental ordeal. By anchoring yourself in the concrete facts of the present moment, you reclaim your peace of mind and stop fighting battles that exist purely in your head.

**Today's Exercise:** Whenever you catch yourself feeling anxious about a potential problem today, pause and ask: *"Is this actual harm happening to me right now, or am I inventing a scenario?"* Write down the single, objective fact of what you are dealing with at this exact moment, and give your energy only to that.
